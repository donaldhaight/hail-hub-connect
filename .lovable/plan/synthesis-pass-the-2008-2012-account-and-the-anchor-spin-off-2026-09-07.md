# Synthesis Pass: The 2008–2012 Account and the Anchor/Spin-Off

A documentation pass, not a build. Fold the founder's 2008–2012 business strategy into the permanent record, then return for the Season 1 systems description.

## What just landed

The Siteforum-era strategy is now described enough to write down:

- **The capture risk.** Insurance companies would only accept a platform if they controlled it. If they controlled it, the platform would become a "cost plus clearinghouse" from the contractor side and "Uber claims" from the claims-management side — a race to the bottom, not a marketplace.
- **The barrier-to-entry move.** Build the platform and each Stakeholder Group, then sell the platform to the Stakeholder Groups rather than to the insurers. The multi-startup / multi-codebase structure was not only legal camouflage; it was architectural redundancy that made the system too distributed for any single player — especially the insurers — to capture.
- **The waiting game.** While the market caught up, the founder tracked blockchain ICOs, SPACs, crypto markets, and later LLM platforms — watching the funding and governance patterns that could host the same idea once the technology and capital were ready.
- **The conflict of interest.** RRCA sits on the opportunity because it is both a construction-management operator and the would-be platform owner. That conflict is not a bug to hide; it is the economic tension the structure must solve. The platform only works if the party building the stack is credibly independent of the party that profits from the projects.
- **What is being sold.** RRCA to the crowd, the FinTech to the crowd, and portions of the remaining Stakeholder Groups to an invited crowd.
