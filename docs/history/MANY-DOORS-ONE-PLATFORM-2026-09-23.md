# Many Doors, One Platform

## The correction

The founder published the work, opened the site, went to the Manual and could not find
what had just been built. An outside reading of the published site followed. It found two
ClaimStore pages saying different things, called the newer work an island, and recommended
that one page win: retire the old one, redirect it, or label it historical.

Both readers — the outside one and this one — then made the same mistake. We assumed that
two pages carrying one venture name were accidental duplication. ADR-028 wrote that
assumption into law: *`/b/<slug>` is retired as an independent destination.* The founder
never asked for that.

> I was looking at Lovable and the code base to do multiple tasks and perform as multiple
> roles of infrastructure. I did not want to change or edit the existing pages. My thoughts
> were landing pages that generated interest that captured and converted an Interested User
> into the platform — as additional and or alternative doors.

The Door was not supposed to replace the house.

## What the surfaces actually are

PrepareAmerica, the Briefing, the Architecture, the brand cards, the Manual and the
application are not competing answers to one question. They are different instruments
performing different jobs:

| Surface | Job |
|---|---|
| PrepareAmerica home | Movement, event, master invitation |
| Briefing and case study | RRCA → correction → ClaimExpress → ClaimStore → USA |
| Architecture | Stakeholder geometry, roles, counties, governance |
| `/b/<slug>` brand cards | The vertical, brand and domain within the architecture |
| Venture landing pages | One market problem, one focused message, one measurable way in |
| Interested User routine | The person's need and their entry context |
| Kimosabe and App Home | The continuing relationship inside the shared platform |
| Manual and corpus | How all of these work together, and who may change them |

`/b/claimstore` explains ClaimStore's position in the Human Blockchain and its relationship
to banking and financial rails. `/claimstore` makes the market case and converts an
Interested User. Both are correct. Both point into the same routine. Neither supersedes the
other. The same applies to SelfInsurity, Buddy Claim, RRCA, Market Applications, Kimosabe
and National Roofing Army.

## What actually caused the confusion

Not duplication. The absence of a label.

Nothing in the product told the founder — or any reader — *this is an architecture card*,
*this is a campaign landing page*, *this is the conversion destination*, *these two pages
express the same venture differently on purpose*, *this one is established infrastructure*,
*this one is a preseason experiment*. Without those declarations, intentional multiplicity
is indistinguishable from an accident.

The remedy is classification and connection, not deletion.

## The completion rule, corrected

The chapter proposed: *a canonical change is incomplete until the previous public expression
is retired, redirected or visibly superseded.* That is superseded by:

> A new Door is complete when its distinct purpose is declared, its relationship to existing
> surfaces is documented, and it connects the Interested User to the shared platform without
> silently replacing another valid expression.

Not *one path, one name, one current page*. Instead:

> Many intentional Doors. Clearly named purposes. One continuing person. One shared platform.

## What the positioning work is for

The seven reports and their landing copy are not surplus. They supply four things the
existing site does not supply consistently: a standalone market case for each venture; a
focused message for the person arriving through that particular interest; a measurable
acquisition Door into the shared platform; and a comparable foundation for the later
portfolio work. They extend the site. They do not rewrite it.

## The reusable journey

    Interest Door → Interested User → Kimosabe → existing identity → App Home

Preserved along the way: the originating Door, the campaign, the promise version, the
expressed interest and the referral source. Role interest stays separate from actual role
and authority, always — ADR-028 §2 is unchanged and is not in dispute.

That preservation is still browser-only. The `entry_context` object remains proposed and
unauthorized (A78). Until it is authorized, the Doors cannot be measured, and the preseason
learning the Doors exist to produce does not accumulate anywhere.

## What was done

- The Surface and Door Registry — every public route, its type, audience, purpose,
  controlling source, call to action, destination, status, whether it captures arrival
  context, and its relationship to other surfaces sharing the venture name. Founder-only,
  at `/admin/surfaces`.
- This ruling, as ADR-029.
- The outside chapter filed verbatim, with its superseded remedy noted and its observation
  preserved.

## What was not done

Nothing was retired, redirected, renamed or rewritten. No navigation changed. No brand card
was touched. No new Door was built. Nothing published. Buddy Claim waits until the registry
has been reviewed — that is the founder's own sequencing, and it is the right one.

## The naming question, left open

The outside reading raised something the correction does not settle: four different things
currently borrow each other's numbers.

1. The five-message sequence — RRCA, case study, ClaimExpress, ClaimStore, United Stakeholders.
2. The seven venture Doors — SelfInsurity, Market Applications, Kimosabe, ClaimStore,
   Buddy Claim, RRCA, National Roofing Army.
3. The founding stakeholder positions — a role geometry, not necessarily companies or Doors.
4. The seventeen-role operating architecture.

Once those are named as four distinct things, the numbers can coexist. Until then, "the
seven" means different things on different pages. Registered as an open question, not
resolved here.

## The standing note

The project stands tall. The work was not lost, not buried, and not overruled. It was
unlabelled — and now it is labelled.
