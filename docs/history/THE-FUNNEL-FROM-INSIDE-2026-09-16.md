# The funnel, from inside the codebase

> **Status:** narrative · **Class:** C2 · **Truth:** `ASSERTION` · **Written:** 2026-09-16

A funnel was narrated from outside the system: nine screens, three audiences, one spine.
This is what it looked like from inside the code that had to carry it.

## The surprise was how little was missing

I expected to find a funnel-shaped hole. I found a funnel with two panes out of it. Arrival
already worked — the front door has been one engine with swappable personas since Buddy
Claim was added, and it hands a stranger an anchor, a holding wallet and an append-only
ledger before asking for a name. Onboarding already worked. App Home already derived work
server-side. The reader already existed as the Owner's Manual. The request already routed
to the founder's queue, where a human — not the form — decides what someone becomes.

What was missing was an offer screen and a place for a person to see their own record.
Both were built at the smallest size that tells the truth.

## The interesting part was what refused to be configuration

Two of the three tracks land somewhere real. The builder and the contractor both converge
on a position the system actually has: request, grant, certification, a fee paid out of a
wallet that already exists. The property owner converges on a property record, and there
is no property record. There is no table, no route, no permission, no object.

I could have built a page that looks like a property file. It would have been the cheapest
screen of the three and the most dishonest thing in the repository. So the owner track
stops at a request and says why, on screen, in the confirmation. A funnel that pretends to
hold something it cannot hold is not a funnel; it is a promise with a form attached.

That is the real finding of this build, and it is architectural rather than editorial: the
three audiences share a spine for eight steps and diverge at the ninth, because two of them
end at a *position* and one of them ends at a *record*. Positions exist. The record is
behind the Records gate, where the locked work order put it.

## What I would not let the framing become

The track a person arrives on is a word in their own browser. It shapes which chapter is
suggested and which offer is shown. It is not written to their record, because the moment
it is written it stops being framing and becomes lead provenance — a first-class field
under ADR-015 with consent attached. Framing copy does not get to introduce a data model
through a side door.

For the same reason, the track and offer travel into the request as free text in a field
that already exists, rather than as new columns. Attribution has a shape now (Observed
Event → Normalized Fact → Attribution Claim → Settlement Decision) and a form is not
allowed to skip to the end of it.

## Where this leaves the confidence

Under ADR-027 the proposition being tested here — *the platform can become an acquisition
engine through content, configuration and routing rather than new architecture* — starts at
zero. This build is a weak evidence event in its favour: the work was mostly configuration,
and the one exception was named rather than papered over. It moves when someone who is not
us walks it and either converts or does not.

Nothing here is a decision. No ADR was written, because a walkable simulation is not a
ruling.
